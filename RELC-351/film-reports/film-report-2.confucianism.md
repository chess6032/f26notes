## Origin

- Confucius
  - 551-479 BCE.
  - Teacher w/ disciples.
  - Advisor to number of gov rulers & officials.
  - Sagehood: One of the sages that are knownin history & religion. He's a sage because of his teachings.

## Nature of God

**Heaven, no god.**

- Confucius himself doesn't believe in a god&mdash;or at least never expressed it. Instead, he discusses a heaven (*tian(1)*).
  - "He learns what is near and aspires to what is lofty, and only *tian* knows him."
    - Even if you go unrecognized by the people around you, *tian* acknowledges & approves (the good) that you are doing.
  - The religious aspect to Confucianism is that there is a "norm set by heaven."
- In general, whether or not Confucianism is a religion is a controversial question.
 
## Nature of Heaven

- "Heaven" is not a great translation of *tian*, which literally translates to "sky."
- *Tian* doesn't have the same connotations that "heaven" has in Western tradition.
  - Not so much describing an *afterlife* where people who have achieved salvation go when they die. It's a more cosmic meaning.
    - It is the cause/source of everything that exists in the cosmic world.
    - It is the practice of virtuous actions in your life that gives you confidence that you're related to *Tian*.
  - *Tian* is related to the concept of *ming*, meaning "mandate". Together, *tian ming* means "heaven's mandate," which roughly describes fate/destiny.
    - If the *Dao* (the "way") proceeds in the world, it is because of "heaven's mandate". If the *Dao* is to be discarded, that too is because of "heaven's mandate".

## Canon

The Five Classics:

1. Spring & Autumn annals.
2. Book of Songs (consisting of ~300 poems).
3. Book of Documents (*shanshu*(?), a collection of documents & speeches attributed to rulers & officials of the early Zhou period&mdash;600 BCE.)
4. Book of Rights (ritual proprieties, very important to self-cultivation in Confucianism.)
5. *Yi jing* (she called it "Book of Divinations," but Wikipedia says it's usually translated as "Book of Changes" or "Classic of Changes." Sometimes it's romanized as *I Ching*.)

## Hierarchy

Hierarchy is focused around "ritual propriety," it appears.

- Rituals/sacrificial ceremonies are commonly performed by rulers.
  - Hence why it's important for rulers to exhibit ritual propriety during these rituals.
  - Hence why it's important for rulers (and people in general) to be pious not only in what they do that is visible to others, but what is visible to all in general.
- Performance of rituals are all-pervasive. One learns how to perform proper rituals from their family, as a child.
- "Secular as sacred" (Herbert (?) in 1972).
  - Everything we do in this world has a sacred aspect because it harmonizes our relationships w/ each other if we conform to ritual proprieties.

## Rites

- Most importal ritual propriety: *filial piety*&mdash;the respect & obedience owed to one's parents.
  - You will obey your parents & extend to them the proper respect & behavior.
  - e.g., you would serve your parents & elders food first during meals.
  - e.g., you would take care of your parents when they grow old.
- The importance is not in going through the motions, but rather how you do it&mdash;the *personal investment*.
  - e.g., Confucius talks about how feeding & sheltering your parents is not the same as feeding & sheltering horses & dogs. 
  - The demeanor & sincerity that you exhibit in your approach & attitude is important.
- Virtue starts with the family, and that in turn enables you to cultivate the more important Confucian virtues.
  - Much of self-cultivation & cultivation of the young consists in getting them to habituate not only the right ritual proprieties but also sincerity when performing these rituals.
    - If you know how to love your parents & elders, that love will extend to the rest of the community, enabling us to have a harmonious relationship of respect & love & care.
  - Virtues:
    - Truthfulness.
    - Reciprocity.
    - Righteousness (doing what is right & appropriate in every situation, as opposed to doing what is benefitting to yourself).
  - Highest virtue: *Ren*, sometimes translated as "benevolence" or "humaneness."
    - This virtue encompasses all other virtues.
  - You extend these virtues gradually, from your family to your community to your country to the whole world&mdash;until, ultimately, you extend it to the rest of the cosmos, where you benefit not only human beings but all living things around us.

## Roles of men vs. women

- Very paternalistic way of life.
- Confucius's writings/teachings rarely mention women.
  - Women suboordinate themselves to men.
- Eventually, Menes, an early practitioner of Confucianism (~400 BCE), places *ren* (and the other cardinal virtues) within human nature, so that human beings can practice these virtues.
  - Menes said we are all born with the "sprouts" of these virtues.

## View of other religions

- Confucianism approves that other religions encourage people to strive to be better.
- The fact that something is a religion would not affect Confucius's approval or disapproval of that thing; his approval would be dependent on whether what one is doing is virtuous or not.

## What sets your faith apart from other religions of the world?

- Confucianism is not a full-fledged religion. It does not have central beliefs about afterlife, god, or salvation.
  - When a Confucianist practices virtuous & morally exemplary living, it is not for the sake of a better afterlife. What is important is *here and now*.
  - Suppose there is some person who is the "most virtuous" to ever live. The significance of their virtue is not observed by a reward to they receive after death, but by the impact they could have on everyone else&mdash;and the cosmos.
  - A practitioner has an impact on their own self-transformation.
