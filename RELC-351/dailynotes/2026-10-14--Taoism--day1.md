# TAOISM

## Taosim is ambiguous

- "Taoism,...like its guiding force [the Tao], defies complete explanation. Its pratitioners would maintain that inexpressibility is completely appropriate."
- An important note is that Taoists are **compfortable with ambiguity.**
  - It's a spiritually weak approach to say that if you can't understand *every* thing about a religion, you can't exercise faith or believe in it.

## Founding

### Founder: Lao Tzu

- *Lao Tzu* means "old boy" or "wise (aged) master".
- May not have actually existed.
- Keeper of the imperial archives.

### Founding

- Lao Tzu had great concerns about the sociopolitical aura of the time.
  - Wars.
  - Poopy govs.
- As he leaves society, a guy is like, "No, wait, write down your philosophy for us!" So he does&mdash;he summarizes the philosophy of each scroll that he kept in his imperial archive&mdash;and then he left on his black ox and was never seen again.
  - Back then, the black ox was considered an untamed animal. As Lao Tzu left, he and his ox faded until they were both invisible, symbolizing that you can tame the untamed animal&mdash;the natural man&mdash;within you.
- Hundreds of years later (142 CE), Lao Tzu appeared to Zhang Daoling, who went on to build Taoism from a philosophy to a fr religion.
  - Ancient Chinese folk religion got intermixed with Taoism philosophy.

### Appeal

- Taosim traditionally appeals to though who are "sick & tired of being sick & tired."

### Denominations

There's ~6 denoms, but they all fall into two large categories: **Philosophical** and **Religious**. These aren't official denominations/congregations, they're different approaches&mdash;but they're still very distinct.

## Nature of God

- *Philosophical* Taoists tend to be atheist/agnostic. Though they believe in the Tao, they don't typically 
- Religious Taoists are somewhat inconsistent in their practices.
  - *Sometimes* polytheists.
  - Nature worship is common.
  - Zoolatry is common.
    - There is doctrine w/in Taoism that states that animals have to be "atoned" for as well. 
  - Don't think in Western terms of Father or Overseer&mdash;the Tao is the only "overseer."

### The Tao

**The Tao is the unproduced producer of all that is&mdash;the source of all things?**

- Has no beginning.
- Omnipresent.
- Exists beyond time.
- Invisible.
- Non-being. It is not a being or a person, it just *is*.
- Indescribable; incomprehensible.

"Taoists have *never* engaged in worship services comparable to those of Christians. The idea that believers should gather on a regular basis and forge or renew a relationship to a higher being is generally alien to Taoism. There has
ever been a Taoist 'Sabbath.'" - Kirkland

### Beyond the Tao

- Religious Taoists believe there are other "gods" beyond the Tao, but "god" is a misnomer. Although they'll speak of them as gods, in truth they're more like Catholic saints that you can petition/appeal to.

## Filial piety

Responsibilities of respect, duty, & obligation to your family.

- Sometimes called "ancestor worship," which isn't quite accurate. They have great respect for their ancestors&mdash;often offered more than Taoist gods&mdash;but they don't worship them as gods.
- Elderly are given the highest respect.
  - They are the reason you exist.
  - They've got street cred from living so long.
  - They've sacrificed so much for you.
    - We are all standing on the shoulders of people we have *never* met (bc they preceded us).
- Reverence for the deceased:
  - Proper cremation (or, rarely, a burial).
  - Proper shrine on their resting place.
  - Remembered&mdash;telling stories, etc.
  - Annual sacrifices on their behalf to ensure they're taken care of wherever they are.

## Soteriology

Not much. They *believe* in an afterlife, but the emphasis is on the mortal life.

The afterlife is wrapped up in the idea of **yin & yang**.
