#### Buddha

- Because of his enlightenment, he is called the "Buddha," meaning "one who is awake."

### Discourse in Deer Park

- After enlightenment, he runs into his five former disciples in a place called Deer Park. 
  - There, he teaches them, and this is said to be the beginning of Buddhism as a religion. Or something.
- After this discourse, he realizes that a life of seclusion *isn't* a noble desire&mdash;he should *spread* the four noble truths.

### Sid's Death

- At 80 yrs of age, Sid died from consuming spoiled pork.
  - He knew the pork was spoiled. A rich man (or smth) served pork to Sid and others, and Sid knew the pork was spoiled. To avoid offending the man by refusing his meal and to save the other people at the table, Sid asked for all the pork to himself. One of Sid's last words to his disciples was asking them to make sure that man didn't feel like he'd done anything wrong.

### Succession and disputes

- Siddartha did *not* appoint a successor, because he felt his teachines themselves were his successor.
  - Buddhism, he believed, did not need a leader, it just needed its four truths.
  - He felt everyone should seek *within* to find the path, rather than relying upon others.
- Sid did not write down any of his teachings, they were all passed orally. As such, after Sid's death, there was lots of doctrinal dispute.
  - 86 denominations formed out of this dispute, of which only one still survives.

### Similarities to Martin Luther the reformer

- Had issues with his particular faith tradition.
  - Corruption among the priests (the Brahmans).
  - Did not believe in the (then) thousands of Gods (now millions).
  - Did not support the caste system.
- Wasn't trying to make a new religion, just trying to reform the one he was in.

## Nature of God

### Is Buddha a God?

- The Buddha is *not* a god.
  - He is venerated, but he's venerated for his teachings and the path to enlightenment he layed out.
- The Buddha is sometimes referred to as a "savior" figure, but they mean he's a *prophetic* figure who brought *saving truths* to the world&mdash;NOT someone who saved the world himself.

### Gods?

- Not all Buddhists understand god in the same way. 
- Therevada Buddhists are basically **non-theistic** (i.e., work out your own salvation&mdash;aside from the gods.)
  - Still makes *many* references to the gods of Hinduism. One can be born into a god-like existence (in one of their lives), but that they are not a creator, savior, or judge.
  - One Thereveda monk once told Gaskill, "Savior beings are for life's cheaters!"
    - It is *your* duty to become enlightened and break attachment. "Quit expecting Mom to pick up your room."
- The Mahayana & Vajrayana traditions believe in many deities, buddhas, & savior beings.

### Purpose of life

1. To reach "Buddha status" through personal purification.
2. Overcome the misery of this world.
    - Sid: "Life is dissatisfaction" (often translated as "pain" or "suffering").
    - Sid: The reason we suffer because we have illusions&mdash;false beliefs&mdash;that we put so much trust in. These illusions are the reason we experience so much suffering.
      - "Life is a long dream. When one finally 'awakens,' or attains Buddhahood, existence is seen for what it is&mdash;a sequence of illusions."
      - "All things are empty and impermanent, so we need to achieve detachment from all things."
      - e.g., being rich makes you happy.
      - e.g., "When I retire..."
      - e.g., being conventionally attractive.
      - e.g., believing your life will be instantly better when you have a car.
    - Similarly, it's taught that everything is related&mdash;similar to Hinduism.

### Reincarnation

- Not all Buddhists believe in reincarnation.
  - Statistically, a lot of *Asian* Buddhists do.
  - e.g., in Tibet, Buddhists there typically believe in *transmigration* of the soul instead.
- If you're talking to a Buddhist, you'd just have to ask them.

### Nature of Heaven

- Heaven: Nirvana
  - Hindus: extinction of your soul.
  - Jain: extinction of attachment.
  - Buddha: It's undefinable.
- Some Buddhists believe in Hell.
- Different denominations have different answers for how to reach Nirvana, though it usually always has something to do w/ karma.
    - Therevada focuses on self redemption. When you die, you want less karma than you have merit, or else you'll come back.
      - "Arhat": Solitary saint (sometimes translated as solitary "worthy").
      - *Theravada* means "extinction," as in you're trying to extinguish all attachment.
    - ^ On the other hand, Mahayana Buddhists believe that's a *terrible* idea. 
      - If you're a *solitary* saint, you're not helping others, so that's actually self-serving. 
      - ^ So instead they focus on being a *Bodhisattva*.
        - Sometimes called "Buddha to be," a *Bodhisattva* refers to someone who has achieved enlightenment but hasn't embraced the last stage of merging w/ Nirvana. Instead, they stay in the cycle as a means of suffering so that they can help limit the suffering of others.
        - This is kind of similar to the LDS idea that your soul can't be saved unless you're trying to save other souls.

## Denominations

Buddhism grew in its early years because it attracted people from Jainism and Hinduism. e.g., more specific doctrine than Jainism, rejected Hinduism's caste system.


1. Theravada (*Hinayana*)
    - Oldest. The only surviving of the original 18. Hence, probably the one that most accurately reflects what Buddha himself taught (acc to historians).
    - Smallest.
    - *Hinayana* means "Way of elders" or something.
    - Mostly in Southern/SE Asia.
2. Mahayana
    - Translation: "Big vehicle" or something, because it suprasses the others in size.
    - Biggest.
    - Philosophy: Religion should update itself as time changes. As people have different needs or views, new principles should be adopted for them.
      - Adopted a number of teachings/practices from other Buddhist & non-Buddhist sects.
      - Hence, some other sects 
    - Founded in ~100 CE, ~600 yrs after the Buddha died.
      - They claim that Buddha secretly taught the Mahayana way to specific, spiritual people, and that's why it was unknown for so long.
    - Believe you can become enlightened without strict asceticism/monasticism.
    - Where: Japan, Korea, Mongolia, Taiwan, China.
    - Sub-denoms(?): Zen & Pure Land.
      - Zen: Very popular in Japan. Meditation is its central tenant. They argue meditation is how you get enlightened because it allows you to be fully in the moment.
        - e.g., if you wear glasses, they become invisible to your eyes&mdash;until someone tells you about your glasses, and then once you're aware of them you can see them again. Zen is designed to help you see things as they are&mdash;to help you see the spectacles you're wearing.
        - ^ Zen has anti-rational rituals ("What is the sound of one hand clapping?" "What did your face look like before your mother was born?" "Without silence or without words, explain the meaning of life." "What is the plot of a self-help book without pages or letters?"). 
        - Goal is NOT to become withdrawan or self-absorbed. Rather, you're using meditation to realize your unknown self (or something).
          - You should not excercise faith in a Buddha, but rather in your own latent Buddha nature.
        - Heavily influenced by Taoism.
      - Pure Land: Common in China.
        - Focuses a lot on grace.
          - Practitioners devote themselves to *Buddha Amitaba*. 
            - They'll say throughout the day, "I take refuge in Buddha Amitabha" (or just chant his name). The idea is he will be touched by your devotion, short-circuit your *karma*, and help you in your enlightenment.
3. Tibetan (*Vajrayana*)
    - Translation: "Vehicle of thunder."
    - Buddhism reached Tibet around the same time Muhammad became a prophet.
    - Not always called Buddhism&mdash;sometimes just called "the best religion" or smth.
    - Believe they're more correct than the others.

### Scripture

Each denom has different canon. But the sayings of the Buddha are common to all denoms.

1. Theravada Canon&mdash;"Tripitaka."
    - Disciple, discourse, and special teaching.
2. Mahayana Canon&mdash;"Chinese canon" (even though it's used outside of China).
    - Modified from the Theravada.
3. Vajrayana&mdash;"Tibetan Canon."
    - Modified from Theravada & Mahayana.
    - Only ones who hold their scripture in ritual esteem. They tend to treat the books themselves as holy, not just its words.
      - They'll pray to them, burn incense to them, etc.

<br/>

- All three sects claim their scriptures can be traced to the Buddha themselves, even though the latter two clearly have texts that originated after Buddha's life.
  - Mahayana claim that those texts were part of the secret teaching during those 600 years.
  - Vajryana claim that Buddha gave those texts post-mortally.
- Buddhism does not have a central commandment to read its scripture.
  - Primary emphasis for scriptures: Monastics.
  - The average Buddhist may know some of the core ones, but many of them are only really read by Monastics.
- For a long time, the scriptural canon was transmitted *orally*.
  - This has the benefit that memorizing and passing down scriptures is "merit".
