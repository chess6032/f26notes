# JUDAISM cont...

## Rites of Passage

### Circumcision

- Who? 
  - Any Jew, born or converted.
- When?
  - When born or converted.
- Why?

Blood must be drawn.

### Bar/Bat Mitzvah

- *Bar Mitzvah* for boys at age 13, *Bat Mitzvah* for girls at 12.
  - Orthodox communities would not do a *Bat Mitzvah*.
- Say passages of Hebrew

### Marriage

- Orthodox communities oft fast on their marriage.
- Traditionally married under a (traditionally white) *chuppah* ("place of protection"), symbolizing that God looks down and agrees on the union.
- It is common (though not obligatory) to hold a 7-night reception.
  - 7 is the Hebrew number of wholeness/completeness.
  - Invite different people each night.

### Death

Seen as a severance.

- In Orthodox traditions, you would rip your garment, symbolizing the severance.
- *Sit shiva*: one week of morning.
  - Dressed in black. (Orthodox).
  - Visitors come by in your home and morn with you and say special prayers.
  - Avoid certain things:
    - Shaving.
    - Sex.
    - Wearing leather.
    - Some Orthodox Jews won't bathe during that time.
    - No parties or joyous shih.

## Sacred Clothing

More pronounced in Orthodox and Reconstructionist communities.

- *Tallit*
  - plus *Tallit Katan*.
- *Kippah* (skull cap&mdash;*Yamakah* in Yiddish). Covering your head shows respect to God.
  - Orthodox Jews might wear this 24/7, even to bed.
- *Tallit*: Boxes worn on the forehead and on the forearm by your heart, with 4 and 1 scrolls, respectively. 
  - Deut. said you should keep God's word at the forefront of your head and close to your heart. Initially, this was interpreted metaphorically, but over time became interpreted literally.

## Buildings

### Temples vs. Synagogues

- Jews today no longer have temples in the same sense they had in ye olden times.
  - Reformed Jews might say our modern synagogues are temples, but Orthodox Jews would be uncomfortable with that, because they'd say modern synagogues and ancient temples serve very different purposes.
- Some small groups say Jews should rebuild the temple.
  - The Temple Institute.
  - Temple Mount & Eretz Yisrael Faithful Movement.

## Prayer

- From the heart *or* memorized.
- Most important prayer in Judaism is the *Shema* (Deut. 6:4).
- *Minyan*: Quorum of 10 adults required for public prayer.
- *Mezuzah*: Prayers inscribed on a door post.
  - Deut. 6:4-9 & 13-31.
  - When walking in/out, you would touch or kiss it.
  - A reminder to pray & keep God's commandments while you're out & about.
  - May be placed on the doorpost of any room in the house except for a bathroom (bc it's not a clean space) or storage closet (bc it's not a lived-in space).
  - Always placed at an angle. This is because Rabbis could not agree whether God wanted it placed horizontally or vertically, so they just went with the middle ground to catch both sides.

## Holidays, feasts, & festivals

Each holiday leads to the next, so you kind of walk through time as you progress through the holidays.

"Jewish Life is structured around the festivals. And thus God is held in constant remembrance throughout the year."

"The home, even more than the synagogue, is the chief center of Jewish life." This echoes what Pres. Nelson said about us needing to be a home-centered religion supported by the church&mdash;not the other way around.

- Notes on Jewish timekeeping:
  - Days are 24 hrs, but start/end at sundown.
  - Use lunar calendar, not solar.

### *Rosh Hashanah*

"New years," emphasizes repentence. (Jews repent all year, but there's especial emphasis during this time.)

- A time of careful introspection. Big focus on examining yourself&mdash;"How have I done this year?"
- Encouraged to put together a court of 3 people who are *painfully* blunt & honest to judge you.
- Jewish tradition says that on *Yom Kippur*, some sins on are *permanently* etched into your book of life. So you especially gotta get those repented for.
- *Shofar* (ram's horn) is blown each morning, symbolizing war against sin.
- FOOD:
  - Nuts = sins! 
    - The ancient Hebrew numbering system used letters, so letters have numerical meaning, and if the sum of the letters in a word equals the sum of another word's, they're related.
      - e.g., Adam (45) - Eve (19) = YHWH (26).
    - Nuts = 17 and sins = 17. So the last thing you should be doing on the "repenting of sins" day is eating something that symbolizes sin.

### *Yom Kippur*

Day of repentance.

- You can gain forgiveness to sins you've committed against God, but not to those you've committed against others.
  - This is because you're face-to-face with God in your prayers, so you can apologize to Him. But if it's a sin against another person, you should go apologize & make amends with them directly if you want God to forgive you.
- Restrictions:
  - No sex. :(
  - Uhhh some other stuff.
- Ye olden times: A goat/lamb was slain on behalf of covenant Israel. On this day only, a priest could enter the holy of holies of the temple with this offering and say God's name.
- *Mikveh*: Ritual immersion.
- *Kol Nidre*: Special hymn that wipes your slate clean of your sins.

### *Sukkot*

Emphasizes God over the world.

- *Sukkah* ("booth"): Family builds a booth in their yard (if they have space) and spends as much time as possible beneath it.
- Booth must be temporary, thus reminding you of the un-dependability of the world.
  - Hence, the booth may not be fixed in the ground.
  - The things you depend on in life come to an end&mdash;oft earlier than you'd like or expect.
- Booth must have a see-through or slotted roof so that you can see the heavens above you, which you *can* depend on.
- In bad weather conditions, you're encouraged to get out of the booth. (This is one of the happier holidays.)
- Common tradition is to set an extra chair and tell the story of&mdash;and maybe invite&mdash;biblical "wanderers."
  - e.g., telling the story of Abraham who smashed his father's idols and then asking to reflect on what idols you have in your life that you should "smash".
- Everyone is expected to bring a *Lulav* to the synagogue.

### *Simchat Torah*

"Reveling with the *Torah*". 

- Marks the end of the public cycle of reading the *Torah*.
- Celebration of covenant Israel's love of God's *word*.
- TRADITIONS:
  - Very celebratory, with spontaneous singing and dancing.
  - Unroll the scrolls and encircle the entire congregation in them, symbolizing the importance of "encircling" our lives in God's word.
  - Passing the scrolls from most respective member of the congregation on and on down to the youngest.
- Only time of the year that a woman can cross into the man's side of the synagogue and hold the leather scrolls.

### *Hanukkah*

"Festival of lights".

- ETYMOLOGY:
  - Alexander the Great conquered the Palestine. Years later, a later king outlaws Jewism, takes over the temple, puts Pagan gods in the holy of holies, forces Jews to sacrifice pigs (which is heresy), worship Pagan gods, etc. Eventually, one family gathered a bunch of Jews and then spent years doing guerilla warfare against the Romans. After seven years they finally kicked out the Romans and took back the temple. They wanted to celebrate, but they only had enough oil to light the *Menorah* for one day. But that one day's worth of oil burned for eight days straight.
- Because Hanukkah is celebrated around Xmas time, it has kind of turned into the Jewish equivalent of Xmas.
  - Gift-giving is common now. Many Jews put up a bush&mdash;akin to a Xmas tree.
- *Menorah*: 6 branches in addition to the central candle. (This is what is placed in temples.)
- *Hanukkiah*: 8 branches in addition to the central candle.
  - (Center branch is the "Helper")
  - One candle is lit each day.
  - You're supposed to put somewhere public, demonstrating your recognition of God's intervention in covenant Israel's history&mdash;and in your own life.
- Games played each night.
  - *Driedel* is used in one of them.
- Central theme of light.
- Just as Hanukkah celebrates the rededication of the temple, on Hanukkah you should rededicate your life.
