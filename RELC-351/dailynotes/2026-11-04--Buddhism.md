## Four Noble Truths

Taught in each branch, but more pronounced in Theravada, since that one hasn't added any new doctrine to its beliefs.

### 1. Life is Pain

Life is pain... life is suffering

- Literal saying was "Life is *dukha* (dissatisfaction (or smth))".
- If you're not currently suffering, chances are someone you love is.
- LDS: As Christians, we are called to take on Christ's name...and Christ suffered.
- Death doesn't end suffering because of reincarnation.

### 2. Pain and suffering are caused by craving, selfish, desire, and attachment

- Gaskill/students alikened this to the idea of "the natural man."
  - If you're addicted to something, it's because you are feeding it to the natural man. 

### 3. Craving and attachment can be eliminated

- Once this is accomplished, *Nirvana* is achieved and suffering ends.

### 4. The "Eightfold Middle Path" leads to the extinction of suffering

Eightfold middle path:

1. Right understanding/view
    - Having the correct understanding in life&mdash;including accepting the 4 noble truths.
2. Right purpose/aspirations/thought
    - Have no ill will towards anyone.
    - Don't have thoughts of "getting back" at someone.
3. Right speech
4. Right conduct/actions
    - The idea that you should be *appropriate* in your thoughts, actions, sexuality, etc.
    - i.e., you don't entertain violent acts, thoughts, or words.
5. Right livelihood
    - An honest day's work for an honest day's pay.
    - ^ and don't do anything that causes pain/suffering/harm.
6. Right effort/endeavor
    - Constantly seek to correct bad habits, morally incorrect behavior, and always seek to practice morally correct behavior.
7. Right alertness/mindfulness
    - Notice, but don't react to what you're noticing.
      - Including allowing yourself to have thoughts that you don't attend to.
    - Be attendand to the moment.
    - Pay attention to details. See things as they really are.
      - LDS: We may be decent at *reading* scripture, but we don't always do great at *noticing* shih from it. Same can be said for our interactions w/ the temple.
    - Attentive to what you're thinking/noticing. This is not a commandment to see every detail of everything (see number 8), but every detail of one thing.
8. Right concentration/meditation
    - "Stable, unified, absorbed attention."
    - Having a singular mind & focus. i.e., not distracted.
    - Controlling monkey mind&mdash;trying to do/think about everything at once.

Whatever you're doing, enlightenment is to be found in that thing. (Can't remember if this was part of #7 or #8.)

- Sid taught that in whatever form you exist, you'll have five elements:
