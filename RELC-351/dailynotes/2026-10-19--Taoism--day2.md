# TAOISM (day 2)

## Philosophical Taoism: *Wu-wei* ("Inaction" or "Unattached Action")

> *"Be content with what you have; rejoice in the way things are. When you realize there is nothing lacking, the whole world belongs to you."* - Lao Tzu

"Unattached action" as in you take action but stay unattached in case what you want doesn't come to pass. That is, you take action but let the universe take its course.

(Gaskill has heard about 67,000 different translations of *wu wei*.)

Found primarily in *philosophical* Taoism.

Principles:

1. Don't resist, confront, or defy.
   1. Embrace what God/the Tao/the universe give it what it may, rather than you insist on an outcome.
      1. "Let God prevail."
2. Don't try to better/change others.
   1. Focus on yourself.
   2. Gaskill talked about a person who asked people near the end of their life what they regret. #1 thing: I wished I'd not put so much energy in trying to change my spouse and kids.
3. Don't exhibit *excessive* ambition, desire, or pride.
   1. If you get cancer, is it OK to get treated? Yes.
   2. You have to give up the world to possess it.
   3. Rather than seeking to change the universe, seek to be *changed by it*.

We should seek the *Tao* ("the way") through *introspection*. At the end of our lives, we ideally should be able to say, "My life was purposeful. I contributed to my community, family, ward, etc. in some meaningful way."  

### Harmony with the Tao

- Principle question of Tao ethics: "If I willed this behavior on the entire world, would the outcome be positive or negative?"
- The experience of the Tao is intuitive beyond words. If you grasp it, that's not the Tao. You just know/feel that you are living with the Tao.
- **Be water, my friends** (Bruce Lee). (If it enters a cup, it becomes the cup. If when flowing it encounters a rock, it goes around the rock.)
  - If you live in harmony with the Tao, nothing can go wrong. All that happens is as it should happen.


### Contrast: Religious Taoism

- Religious Taoism teaches the *opposite* of the *Wu-Wei*.
  - e.g., they sought to invent the "Elixir of Immortality" or proverbial "fountain of youth" through herbs.
- ^ Hence why religious Taoism is the *yang* between the two&mdash;aggressive&mdash;while philosophical Taoism is *yin*&mdash;passive
- Philosophical Taoists think of the Tao as a force, while Religious Taoism thinks of the Tao as a god.

## Scriptural canon

- One of the largest scriptural canon of any (existing) religion.
- Canon is *growing*.
  - Special groups of priests (*Tangji*&mdash;"spirit writers" in the textbook) can receive inspiration in the temples. Then they'd publish it on websites (or, in the past, ????), and if it becomes widely accepted out in the rest of the Taoist community, it becomes accepted as canon.

### Main Scriptures

- MOST IMPORTANT: *Tao Te Ching*
  - Attributed to Lao Tzu.
  - Summary of the message of the 81 scrolls of which Lao Tzu was the curator.
    - In this sense, Lao Tzu can be alikened to Mormon.
  - Gaskill compared the Tao (or smth else idk) to the "Light of Christ". 
- SECOND MOST IMPORTANT: *Chuang-tzu*.
  - Teaches that knowledge can be deceptive and right/wrong is not black/white.
  - Examples of non-black-and-white morality:
    - e.g., if you were in WWII and you're a Christian hiding Jews and you lie to the Gestapo... you broke the commandment not to lie, but you were following a higher ethic.
    - e.g., at the end of WWII, the US ends WWII by dropping atomic bombs on Japan. However, in doing so, this killed 247K non-combatants.
  - Example of knowledge being deceptive&mdash;from Gaskill's personal experience. 
    - Gaskill once saw what he thought was one of his sons (Gary) stabbing his brother (Keaton) with a pair of scissors. In reality, Keaton had tripped while holding scissors and Gaskill walked in right as Gary was pulling the scissors out of Keaton's head.
      - Part of the reason Gaskill jumped to this conclusion was because the prior week, Gary was doing the dishes and threw a steak knife at Keaton's head.
  - **Your perception of the truth is not as cut-and-dry as you think.**
    - **Don't doubt your faith because you think you see more than you do.**
- OF THE FIVE CLASSICS: *I Ching* ("Book of Changes")
  - Helps you find answers to your questions through spirituality.
    - That is, the *I Ching* is a form of divination/revelation.
  - Contains 64 hexagrams. After asking a question, you do one of two things to determine hexagram&mdash;pulling sticks associated with hexagrams or flipping coins to build one&mdash;and then you read the text associated with the hexagram, which supposedly contains the answer to your question.
  - Used by Confucianists as well.
    - Confucius said that if he had 50 yrs left to live, he would dedicate them to studying the *I Ching*. (Or smth like that idk.)

## Symbols

### Yin/Yang

- Most common *visual* symbol.
- Note the "eyes" is central to the symbol. 
  - While yin or yang is more dominant within you, the other is present as well. If you're living w/ the Tao, the two opposites are getting closer together. Forgetting the "eyes" misses this.

### Water

- Lowest possible position.
  - Emphasizes submission.
- Can be very powerful.
- Futile to struggle against it.
  - If a flood comes, the answer is not to try to swim against it. The answer is to go with the flow. Similarly, if you do not conform to the Tao, it will overcome you; so allow it to take you where it will.
  - Confrom to it or be destroyed.

### Dietary Restrictions

- Most prominent in the *Hygiene school* of Taoism.
  - Can't consume meat or wine because the 36,000 gods inside you don't like it.
- Fruitarians: Can't eat grain because it feeds the worms in your head, heart, and abdomen that cause aging.

These emphasize how religious Taoists focus a lot on preserving their life.

### *Wu-hsing*: the 5 elements of nature

> *(Not to be confused with [Wu-Wei](#philosophical-taoism-wu-wei-inaction-or-unattached-action))*

1. Earth
2. Metal
3. Water
4. Wood
5. Fire

- According to Taoism, these are the five fundamental elements of nature.
- Highlights the uber-interconnectedness of all things.
  - Nothing is coincidental. Everything that is happening is the result of interactions you can't see.
  - e.g., a volcano in the 1800s caused Joseph Smith Sr.'s farm to fail, so he moved to Palmyra, where Joseph Smith Jr. received the First Vision.
  - e.g., Caleb met Alena through which he met Lily, then met Isaac/Cruz through which he met Miles, and now Miles & Lily are dating. All because Caleb is a procrastinator and kept procrastinating signing up for housing and ending up in Heritage building 15. Silly guy.

### Religious Taoism: Spirits

- Religious Taoists believe spirits exist everywhere. There are good spirits&mdash;*Shen*&mdash;and bad spirits&mdash;*Kuei*.
  - Ancient Chinese believed you had to do things to appease the spirits around you&mdash;good and bad. e.g., Chinese resturaunts today have a table where they put food as an offering.
- Religious Taoists have a lot of traditions/ceremonies to scare away bad spirits.
  - e.g., fireworks.
  - e.g., exorcisms.
