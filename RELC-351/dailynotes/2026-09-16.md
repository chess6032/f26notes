# JUDAISM

> [!NOTE]
> "Jud-*ah*-ism", not "Judy-ism".

## Origins 

*What provoked the creation of this faith?* Genesis 15, God approaches Abraham.

- Founder: Abraham.
  - It is with him who God made covenants with.
  - Different for us LDS folks, who would say that it originated in the pre-existence.
- Part of being "chosen" is separating yourself from the world.
  - "Depart from the tents of wicked men, lest ye be consumed in all their sins" (Moses, Numbers 16:26).

## Denominations

- Most depictions of Jews depict them as Haredi or Modern Orthodox (the heavily observant Jews), but they comprise only 4 million of the 13-14 million Jews.
- About 1/3 of Jews live in Israel or Palestine.
- Denominations are not centralized in the way the LDS church is. There are accredited bodies and seminaries, but generally speaking, each congregation is inidividually managed.
- Religious scholars generally agree that the biggest thread to Judaism is assimilation/westernization.

### Haredi

Sometimes referred to as "Ultra Orthodox," but they themselves don't like that term. (It sounds akin to smth like "flaming liberal.")

- POPULATION: 1m (4%)
- "Haredi" means "one who trembles."
- These folks want to be *completely detached* from the modern world.
- Secular employment is out of the question, so they oft live in pockets and work for each other.
- Strictly observant of both written and oral *Torah*.
  - Oral *Torah*: God gave Moses 600-ish rules, only writing down 10 of them.
  - Based on one Gaskill is saying, a lot of the rules appear to originate as rules to separate the Jews from their Pagan neighbors, but have persisted and evolved into strict by-the-letter rules.
- Consider Rabbis on the same level as prophets of old.

### Modern Orthodox

- POPULATION: 3m (22%)
- Biggest difference btwn Haredi:
  - Looks.
  - How they integrate themselves in the world.
    - They would say they are called to be *leavened* within the world (or smth like that). So if they can find a moral employer and can do some shih that improves the world, they would

Does "Orthodox" refer to Haredi or modern Orthodox Jews? If you're seeing the word used in media, chances are the source doesn't even understand the difference.

### Conservative

"Conservative" as in "conserving on how Jewish we are." This is where progressivism starts.

- POPULATION: 4.6m (33%)
- More comfortable with tweaking tradition to be more modern or flexible.
  - Both men & women are allowed to read from the Torah in the synagogue.
  - You can drive to the synagogue on the sabbath.
  - Some congregations (but not all) allow female Rabbis.

### Reform

- POPULATION: 5.3m (38%) 
  - (Highest-population denomination.)
  - High perentage of agnostics/atheists.
    - Might say, "I believe in Judaism for its principles and purpose&mdash;and I may even believe in some higher power&mdash;but I wouldn't believe that there is a single deity/being." 
- Happily throw out Jewish traditions that don't make sense.
- Men & women are fully equal. As a consequence, they have *more* female than male rabbis.
  - An interesting thing: Women rabbis will oft wear rabbi clothing to stand out as rabbis, even though in Jewish law men & women aren't supposed to cross dress.
- In the US, worship services are held in English&mdash;and may sometimes even feel protestant.

### Reconstructionist

Born out of the Holocaust, which made many Jews question their belief in god as a whole. But some guy Whitney, knowing that Judaism is not just a religion but also a nationality, wanted to create a movement for those Jews to keep Jewish culture alive.

- POPULATION: 60K (0%)
- Focus on *being* Jewish as population, not on beleiving in a Jewish god. Still do Jewish *practices* (festivals, worship services, etc.) without subscribing to Jewish *beliefs*.
  - e.g., do readings of the Torah, but strip out any references to a god.
- Laws & commandments are no longer binding.
  - "Post-hollolick"
  - To be completely inclusive, you can't have any rules or laws.
- *If* there is a god, reconstructionists would say they are not anthropromorphic or conscious.
  - God can't hear or answer prayers, and therefore can't give revelation or scripture.
- Prefer Jewish cultural centers instead of synagogues.

### Assimilated

*Hiloni* means "secularized". These folks 

- POPULATION: 4.2m
- People who do not embrace being Jewish at all. Have severed all their connection to Jewish identity.
- *Heavily* atheist.
- Likely antagonist to the idea of organized religion as a whole.

## Nature of God

God commanded to not write His name, so Jews write G-D or G0D&mdash;even more progressive ones.

- Mileiu&mdash;Polythesitic.
- There's evidence in the Hebrew bible that early Jews were more likely Mono*latrist* than Monotheism&mdash;they believe there exist multiple gods but they only worship one.
  - e.g., Abraham used "Gods" in Abraham 4:24-27, but fought against the polytheism (worshipping multiple dieties) of his father.
- After influence from Zoriastranism, Jews leaned towards mono*theist*&mdash;the belief that their only exists one god, which they worship. By New Testament times, Jews were basically fully monotheoist.

### Messiah

The concept of a Messiah has long been part of Jewish belief. But not all agree what that would look like:

- A religious leader?
- Military/political leader?
- Metaphor or symbol?
- The founding of the State of Israel?

As time progresses, fewer and fewer Jews believe that the Messiah will be an actual individual.

> [!NOTE]
> The textbook claimed that all Jews believe the Messiah will be a person. That's not true now, and it probably wasn't even true in the 1970s when the textbook was written.

"In Judaism, the Messiah is *not* the Redeemer; God is the Redeemer."

## Sripture

- **T**a**n**a**k**h: The Law (*Torah*), Prophets (*Neviim*), & Writings (*K'tuvim*).
- Talmud: Gemara + Mishnah.
  - Mishna: The Oral Law or "Oral Torah" received by Moses on Sinai.
  - Gamera: Analysis & commentary on the Mishnah.
- Midrash: Commentary on *any* portion of the **T**a**n**a**k**h or Hebrew bible.

> [!NOTE]
> Don't use the term "Old Testament." (That's like calling the Book of Mormon "the golden bible.")
>
> Use terms like "Jewish canon," "Hebrew bible," etc.
