# Cautions when learning about other religions

## Avoid "why" questions

e.g., "Why do you believe X? Why do you do Y?"

- Reason:
  - Ignorance: Not all members of a religion do the same thing.
  - It oft is taken as "Why in the world do you...?"
- Do ts instead:
  - Use "how" or "what" questions instead. e.g., "How do you use scripture in your faith? What is you relationship with God?"
  - Preface with something positive.

## Avoid media sensationalism

If you're getting your info from the media (or internet), it's most likely wrong, skewed, out of context, not the full truth, etc.

- Remember, media outlets want your clicks. They're not as concerned w/ accuracy with how they present a religion as they are getting you to listen to them.
- e.g., Warren Jeffs, one of the leaders for the FLDS community, is now serving in prison for forcing tweens to marry old men. In an interview with a "cult expert", he kept having to correct his interviewer that members of the FLDS church are not the same as the "mormons" of the mainstream LDS church.
  - "Mormon" is sensational.

## Don't stare

Don't stare at those who are different. It's rude.

## Avoid loaded terms

- e.g. "cult," "sect," "mainstream," "traditional," "orthodox," "normal".
  - "cult" is theologically and linguistically stupid. "Cult" is Latin for "they who believe in the Gods." So etymogically, "cult" refers to anyone, pretty much. So the connotation it carries today is very flimsy and ill-defined.
- It builds walls, not bridges. It's not productive dialogue.

## Be realistic about cultural appropration

Be realistic about "cultural appropriation".

- Cultural appropriation: Inappropriately stealing a piece of culture from another race or ethnic group, oft for gain (e.g. entertainment).
  - Example of cultural appropriation: Rachel Dolezel, a white woman who presented herself (colored her skin, styled her hair), and became a officer in the NAACP. She presented herself as black to rise through the ranks in a black-only organization.
  - NOT an example of cultural appropriation: A yoga class for people with physical disabilities. Protests errupted because they felt yoga is Hindu and you can't do a Hindu thing for white people. (Or something like that, I didn't catch the full story.)
- Cultural appropriation about religion is an entirely different thing. Gaskill appears to argue that you can't really appropriate a religion.
  - Who owns meditation or prayer or fasting?
    - **"That which is good cometh of God" (Moroni)**.
  - The gospel of Jesus Christ *encourages* you to "culturally appropriate" other religions.
  - e.g., when Gaskill left Orthodoxy and converted to the church, he had to "appropriate" a new culture. There were a lot of things he had to embrace and learn.
  - e.g., a Hindu friend said Gaskill was reincarted into the wrong religion: He does better being Hindu than most Hindus do.

## Don't step on already bruised toes

Don't step on already bruised toes.

- e.g., asking an LDS, "How many moms do you have?"

# Showing respect to other religions

## Six principles (posted by the church)

### 1. Treat their religious articles & practices w/ respect

- Offer to other religions the *same* respect you would want them to offer us.
- Gaskill gave some examples of Mormon missionaries doing crazy disrespectful shih.

### 2. Observe their customs when possible and appropriate

- If you're going to be in a sacred location or worship service, you should engage.
  - Don't stand there and gawk. Observe.
  - e.g., going to a mosque, remove your shoes before entering.
  - e.g., if you're a male going into a Catholic cathedral in Latin America, take your hat off.

### 3. Never ridicule

- e.g., Romans believed early Christians were sexual deviants and cannibals. (Had to do w/ "agape", the word early Christians used for their services.)

## Keys in understanding other religions

### 1. All theology comes from questions we have

- If you see some doctrine in a religion and it doesn't make sense to you, first figure out what theological question it's asking and you'll find it makes much more sense.
- e.g., reincarnation in Hinduism is *not* answering questions about the afterlife&mdash;the afterlife is *Nirvana*. Reincarnation is answering the question that Hindus believe you are perfect when you die, but people don't seem so perfect when they die.

### 2. Jehovah's Witnesses and LDSaints are unique

- Most religions aren't doctrinal or dogmatic.
- Most religions believe whatever they want&mdash;and don't worry about "orthodoxy" in beliefs or "orthopraxy" in practices.

### 3. Keep an open mind & be objective

Don't view other religions through the lenses of your own.

- (Temporarily) set your personal lenses on the shelf!
- You *can't* understand another religion if you try to see it through the lenses of your own religion or your own personal experiences.
